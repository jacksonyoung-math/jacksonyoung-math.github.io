RESEARCH

My research, broadly, is focused at the intersection of arithmetic lattices and hyperbolic geometry. A lattice is a discrete subgroup (of a semisimple Lie group) having finite covolume. An arithmetic lattice is a lattice that is in some sense the "integer points" of a semisimple Lie group. For example, $\mathbb{Z}^n < \mathbb{R}^n$ is arithmetic, because its quotient is the $n$-torus, which is compact and thus finite volume. A more complicated example is the special linear group $\text{SL}(n; \mathbb{Z})$ in $\text{SL}(n; \mathbb{R})$. On the other hand, hyperbolic geometry is the geometry of negatively curved spaces. There is a lot of rich geometric information that falls out of this largely number-theoretic construction, especially when the symmetric space it lives in is negatively curved. The geometric structures of hyperbolic and complex hyperbolic geometry provide a rich array of arithmetic lattices to study and learn from. Right now, I am thinking about how to get group presentations for certain arithmetic lattices in the context of complex hyperbolic geometry; that is, lattices in the group of isometries of complex hyperbolic $2$-space $H_\mathbb{C}^2$.
